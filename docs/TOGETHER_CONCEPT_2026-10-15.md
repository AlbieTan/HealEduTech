# Together: shared learning and companion concept

**Status:** UI concept on `codex/together-avatar-concept`; no points, rewards, avatar growth, learner submissions, or publication are implemented.

## Intended learner journey

1. Choose a book or activity with a friend.
2. Try an idea, compare observations, and make a small record.
3. Discuss a discovery with a facilitator before any optional sharing.

The current Together page contains fictional showcases and a page-only project preview. This draft adds a clearer invitation to collaborate and an original pixel-art forest explorer. The character is illustrative. It does not represent a student's account or progress.

## Why the companion is optional

The 10–16 age range spans different tastes and levels of independence. A character may make progress feel visible for some learners, while a fixed mascot or public score may feel childish or competitive to others. The first test should let learners choose whether to use a companion and gather feedback from younger and older students separately.

If EVG later approves recognition, link visual growth to reviewed learning behaviours such as trying a new activity, explaining a discovery, or helping a peer. Prefer private milestones and cosmetic choices. Do not award points for time spent, repeated clicks, or raw quantity of posts. Avoid leaderboards, point deductions, streak pressure, and public comparison. The avatar should never gate books, activities, feedback, or sharing.

This is a design recommendation, not a proven effect in EVG's setting. A quasi-experiment with 133 eighth-grade students found higher motivation and course satisfaction in a gamified learning environment but no significant achievement effect. A randomized study with undergraduates found higher learning in a combined game-element condition, but no significant intrinsic-motivation differences; its age group and intervention do not establish outcomes for EVG learners. See [the eighth-grade study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2022.e10249) and [the randomized trial](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40692-025-00366-x).

## Review questions for EVG and students

- Does the shared activity → discovery → facilitator conversation path make sense outside school?
- Does the character appeal to both 10–12 and 13–16 year olds? Would they rather choose a different visual style or opt out?
- Which actions genuinely show learning or support for others, and who can verify them fairly?
- What visibility should a learner, facilitator, and peers have into any future progress?
- Can students understand that the current project form does not save or publish their work?

## Artwork

`public/images/together/forest-explorer-concept.png` was generated with the built-in Image Gen tool as an original transparent pixel-art concept. Prompt: “Single original pixel-art avatar concept for the Together tab of an EVG Vietnam educational site serving ages 10–16. An age-neutral tiny forest explorer creature, leafy cloak and field notebook, expression of curiosity and kindness. Crisp square pixels, game sprite style, clean silhouette, forest green/sage/warm cream/tan/muted gold palette. Full body centered, transparent background, no text, no logo, no badge, no points, no ClassDojo resemblance. Readable at 96 px.”

Before adopting this artwork for a production avatar system, check legibility at small sizes and test its tone with EVG learners. A deterministic sprite set with consistent poses and an opt-out would be needed for actual progression.
